## Key Statistics
- **#1 cranberry-producing state** in the U.S. (since 1994, 31+ consecutive years)
- **~60% of all U.S. cranberries** produced in Wisconsin
- **Nearly 5.5 million barrels** produced in 2024 (peaked at 6.1M in 2016)
- **6.1 million barrels** in 2023 (reported as last year's crop in 2025 article)
- **5.3 million barrels** forecasted for 2025
- **20,000+ acres** in production
- **20 counties** (primarily central Wisconsin; Wood and Jackson counties produce the most)
- **250+ cranberry farms** (mostly family-owned)
- **~$1 billion** annual economic impact
- **~4,000 jobs** supported
- **Cranberries are Wisconsin's #1 fruit crop** (both quantity and value)

## Funding
- Funded through grower assessment: **25 cents per barrel** (raised from 10 cents for 2025)
- Generates approximately **$546,000–$577,000** annually (expected to increase with new rate)
- Board committed **$577,138** in 2025 grant funding
  - 9 research projects
  - 5 education and communication programs
  - $339,290 invested in education and promotional programs

## History
- Cranberry Board started in **1983** as a market order board
- Wild cranberries are native to Wisconsin marshlands
- Native Americans (Ho-Chunk) harvested for centuries (food, medicine, dye)
- Commercial production began near **Berlin, Green Lake County in 1850s**
- Industry centered around **Tomah, Warrens, and Wisconsin Rapids**
- Wisconsin became #1 producer in **1994** (surpassing Massachusetts)
- WSCGA founded in **1887**
- 1867: State legislature passed Cranberry Law (rights to construct dams for bogs)
- Some marshes have been in production for **100+ years**
- Cranberries originally stored/shipped in 100-lb wooden barrels (still the unit of measurement)

## Research Partnerships
- **UW-Madison** — Fruit crop specialists, plant breeding (HyRed variety), pest management
- **WCREF** (Wisconsin Cranberry Research and Education Foundation)
  - Owns/operates Wisconsin Cranberry Research Station near Black River Falls
  - Purchased property in 2017, construction completed 2022
  - Public/private partnership with USDA-ARS and UW-Madison
  - Hosts ~12+ research projects per season
- **USDA Agricultural Research Service (ARS)**
- **VacCAP** — $12.8M effort to improve cranberries and blueberries

## Research Focus Areas
- Cold hardiness of different cranberry varieties
- Sustainable growing practices
- Pest and disease management
- Crop yield and quality improvement
- Plant physiology and nutrient efficiency
- New variety development (hardy, high-yield)
- Climate change adaptation
- Targeted fertilizer/pesticide use
- New food products and uses
